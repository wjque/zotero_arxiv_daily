# v0.2.0 Personalized Ranking Quality and Efficient LLM Refinement

- **Target version:** v0.2.0
- **Status:** Active
- **Created:** 2026-08-02
- **Last updated:** 2026-08-02
- **Project timezone:** Asia/Shanghai
- **Predecessor:** [v0.1.2 Profile Snapshot and Compact Status](v0.1.2-profile-snapshot-site-density.md)

## Objective

Improve recommendation quality and cost efficiency across both deterministic coarse ranking and
LLM-assisted fine ranking. The system must learn a richer, privacy-bounded representation of the
user's interests from the complete local Zotero library, generalize explicit reading feedback to
future papers, evaluate scientific-quality evidence without confusing popularity with quality, and
produce more factual summaries and more specific recommendation reasons with fewer wasted model
tokens.

The release is evaluation-led. New signals and weights are not promoted because they appear
plausible; they must improve a frozen v0.1.2 baseline on held-out or temporally replayed user labels
while preserving diversity, privacy, failure safety, and an explicit model-cost budget.

## Product Outcomes and Acceptance Criteria

### Recommendation quality

- A reproducible offline evaluator freezes the v0.1.2 ranker as a baseline and reports at least
  coarse-stage Recall@K, Precision@20, NDCG@20, negative-label rate at 20, source/category coverage,
  intra-list diversity, and score calibration when enough labels exist.
- On the agreed held-out evaluation set, coarse Recall@60 is no worse than the v0.1.2 baseline and
  reaches at least 95% of labeled relevant papers when the sample size supports that estimate.
- Fine-ranked NDCG@20 improves by at least 0.05 absolute over the frozen baseline without increasing
  the held-out negative-label rate. If fewer than 40 independent labels are available, the result is
  reported as provisional and cannot trigger automatic weight tuning.
- Final ranking remains deterministic for identical profile, feedback, evidence, prompt, model, and
  candidate inputs. Every score is decomposable into versioned components and evidence availability.
- Exploration remains bounded and measurable. At least one slot is available to eligible adjacent or
  exploratory work when quality thresholds permit, while explicit negative preferences and repeat
  suppression remain effective.

### Profile and feedback quality

- The local profile distinguishes long-term interests, recent interests, methods/tasks/domains,
  watched identities, and confidence in the evidence that produced each preference.
- Zotero library presence alone is treated as a weak positive. Manual tags, collection membership,
  annotations, annotation comments, and explicit curated labels receive separate inspectable weights.
- Feedback generalizes beyond a previously seen arXiv ID through bounded topic, method, author,
  institution, and quality-dimension aggregates with decay and minimum-sample guards.
- Impressions and explicit outcomes are distinguished so that missing feedback is never interpreted
  as negative feedback. Position and exposure bias are visible in evaluation manifests.
- All weight sets have an immutable version and can be rolled back independently of schemas and code.

### Scientific-quality assessment

- The quality contract separates at least contribution clarity, novelty, insight plausibility,
  methodological evidence, empirical evidence, limitations, and reproducibility/open-source evidence.
- Every quality dimension supports `unknown` or unavailable evidence. Missing a repository never
  penalizes a theoretical or otherwise non-software paper merely because the signal is inapplicable.
- An open-source signal uses a verified paper-to-repository association and bounded structured facts
  such as license presence, archived status, release/artifact availability, documentation, and recent
  maintenance. Stars, citations, venue, author, and institution prestige are never dominant quality
  signals and are age/field normalized if used at all.
- LLM novelty and insight judgments include bounded evidence references to supplied fields and an
  uncertainty value. Abstract-only claims are presented as assessments, not verified facts.

### LLM quality and efficiency

- Fine ranking and prose generation use separate versioned contracts or an empirically justified
  single-pass alternative. The selected design generates full summary/reason prose only for papers
  that can enter the final batch.
- The model receives a bounded, allowlisted preference context and quality rubric, never raw Zotero
  notes, annotations, PDF text, private curated reasons, or browser feedback prose.
- Summaries identify the problem, approach, and principal claimed result when evidence exists.
  Recommendation reasons name the relevant user-interest signal and the paper-specific contribution;
  generic restatements of the abstract fail validation.
- Median model output tokens per deployed recommendation fall by at least 25% from the measured
  v0.1.2 baseline, while input tokens, requests, latency, cache hits, retries, and estimated/actual
  cost are included in the non-sensitive run manifest.
- Provider failure, enrichment failure, invalid structured output, an empty shortlist, and a repeated
  run preserve the previous usable site and do not advance feedback, history, or learning state.

### Release and safety

- All new persisted and transported schemas have exact backward readers or explicit migrations with
  fixtures. A v0.1.2 state can run under v0.2.0 without silently reinterpreting old fields.
- Full formatting, lint, strict typing, offline tests, migrations, package build, static-site build,
  artifact inspection, shadow evaluation, and an approved production canary pass before release.
- Browser artifacts and logs contain no raw Zotero data, private labels, free-text feedback, secrets,
  full prompts, or unbounded external-provider responses.

## Current Baseline and Identified Gaps

The v0.1.2 system provides safe foundations but does not yet support the target quality loop:

- Profile construction counts normalized tokens from titles, abstracts, dates, manual tags, notes,
  highlights, and comments. It does not compute true time decay, semantic similarity, document
  frequency, or distinct method/task/domain facets. The protected projection drops term weights.
- Representative Zotero papers are reduced to year, a few terms, and inferred categories. Their role
  as weak library samples, strong annotation-backed interests, or explicit exemplars is not retained.
- Local coarse ranking is a fixed sum of lexical overlap, category, 14-day recency, per-paper feedback,
  and capped watched-identity bonuses. Component ranges are not normalized, so adding more weights
  directly would make calibration and interpretation progressively worse.
- Feedback is an additive adjustment keyed only by arXiv ID. It cannot learn that the user likes a
  topic, method, author, institution, type of contribution, or reproducibility characteristic.
- The LLM sees public candidate metadata and only the first 400 abstract characters. It receives no
  user preference context or detailed quality rubric and returns one undifferentiated quality number,
  plus summary and reason prose for every coarse candidate.
- Quality metadata such as verified code, artifact status, citation neighborhood, venue, or
  retraction status is unavailable. The current candidate model cannot express evidence provenance,
  applicability, freshness, or uncertainty.
- There is no frozen labeled evaluation set, temporal replay, shadow comparison, calibration report,
  or metric gate. Production feedback alone is sparse and affected by rank/exposure bias.

## Scope

### In scope

- A versioned offline evaluation harness, frozen v0.1.2 baseline, replay fixtures, metric reports,
  weight-set registry, and comparison gates.
- Incremental local Zotero feature extraction and a richer privacy-bounded interest profile.
- A versioned feedback event ledger, impression tracking, delayed outcomes, reason codes, aggregation,
  decay, and migration from feedback state v1.
- Normalized deterministic coarse-ranking features, quality thresholds, configurable group caps,
  diversity, exploration, and stable ordering.
- Optional public-paper evidence enrichment behind replaceable cached adapters.
- Provider-neutral LLM judge and explanation contracts, prompt versions, cache namespaces,
  uncertainty handling, and cost accounting.
- A local-only curated evaluation/exemplar corpus format and an importer that can reuse dedicated
  Zotero collections/tags instead of requiring a second manually maintained bibliography.
- Publishable quality explanations and evidence badges only when they can be represented safely,
  concisely, and without exposing private profile details.
- Schema migrations, configuration, operational documentation, canary deployment, and rollback.

### Non-goals

- Training or fine-tuning a foundation model in v0.2.0.
- Building a vector database, queue, background service, multi-user recommender, or browser-side model.
- Sending raw Zotero notes, annotations, PDF text, curated free-text reasons, or complete local profiles
  to GitHub Actions or a model provider.
- Automatically downloading or parsing arbitrary repository source trees or executing paper code.
- Treating citation counts, stars, venue prestige, famous authors, or institution reputation as proof
  of correctness, novelty, or relevance.
- Claiming that an LLM has verified mathematical correctness, experimental reproducibility, or true
  novelty from an abstract.
- A broad website redesign. Only feedback controls and explanation fields required by the learning
  and evaluation loop are included; larger information-architecture work belongs to a later plan.
- Fully autonomous online weight optimization. v0.2.0 may propose evaluated weight changes, but
  production activation remains explicit and versioned.

## Extension-versus-Refactor Assessment

A refactor is required before adding new weights. Extending the current fixed score sum would mix
signals with incompatible scales, duplicate profile and feedback interpretation, and make missing
evidence indistinguishable from negative evidence. The single LLM proposal schema also couples
quality judging to prose generation, which wastes output tokens and prevents independent evaluation.

The refactor will preserve characterized v0.1.2 behavior while introducing these boundaries:

1. Typed normalized feature values with `value`, `applicable`, `confidence`, `provenance`, and
   `observed_at` semantics.
2. A versioned weight set that combines feature groups locally and records every contribution.
3. Separate selection, evidence enrichment, LLM judgment, explanation generation, and presentation.
4. Append-only feedback events separated from derived aggregates and activation checkpoints.
5. Offline evaluation that consumes immutable snapshots and never mutates production state.

Temporary compatibility code must have a removal milestone. The v0.1.2 scorer remains available only
as the frozen baseline and rollback adapter, not as a second production path after v0.2.0 release.

## Proposed Architecture

### Data flow

```text
Local Zotero sync
  -> versioned item evidence and curated labels (local only)
  -> long-term/recent preference model
  -> bounded protected profile

Public arXiv candidate pool
  -> deterministic feature extraction and coarse ranking
  -> top-K public evidence enrichment
  -> compact LLM quality judgment
  -> local weighted selection, diversity, and exploration policy
  -> LLM summary/reason generation for final papers only
  -> encrypted static site

Impressions and explicit feedback
  -> validated append-only feedback events
  -> decayed, minimum-sample preference aggregates
  -> next profile/ranking snapshot after successful activation
```

### Module ownership

- `profile/` owns local Zotero evidence, incremental feature extraction, long/recent preference
  construction, exemplar projection, and the protected-profile allowlist.
- `feedback/` owns immutable event schemas, validation, aggregation, decay, reason categories, and
  activation checkpoints. It does not calculate final ranking scores.
- `ranking/` owns normalized feature contracts, weight sets, coarse scoring, diversity, exploration,
  selection, score explanations, and the frozen v0.1.2 baseline adapter.
- `evidence/` may be introduced when the first real public-metadata adapter exists. It owns
  provider-neutral public evidence models, repository association, provenance, caching policy,
  throttling, and provider adapters. Ranking consumes validated evidence models, not HTTP clients.
- `llm/` owns provider-neutral judge/explanation requests, structured response validation, prompt
  versions, token/cost accounting, and replaceable providers.
- `evaluation/` may be introduced with the real offline evaluator. It owns immutable datasets,
  temporal splits, metrics, comparison reports, and release gates; it cannot write production state.
- `pipeline/` coordinates the stages and checkpoints only. It does not parse providers or contain
  feature formulas.
- `storage/` owns explicit SQLite/JSON migrations, atomic snapshots, and rollback-safe activation.
- `site/` receives only publishable explanations, public evidence, and categorical feedback controls.

### Versioned ranking contract

Each candidate feature is normalized to `[0, 1]` before weighting and carries applicability and
confidence. Negative preferences use an explicit bounded penalty rather than overloading a missing
value. The initial feature groups are:

| Group | Candidate signals | Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interest relevance | Long-term similarity, recent similarity, topic/category, method/task/domain, representative-paper similarity | Library presence is weak; annotation/comment and curated evidence are stronger; recent and long-term interests are kept separate |
| Explicit preference | Watched identities, positive/negative topic and method aggregates, read/worthwhile outcomes | Time decay, minimum samples, per-group caps, and no inference from missing feedback |
| Scientific quality | Contribution clarity, novelty, insight plausibility, methodological/empirical evidence, limitations | LLM evidence required; unknown is distinct from low; abstract-only confidence is capped |
| Reproducibility | Verified repository, license, artifacts/releases, documentation, maintenance | Applied only when relevant; repository association must be verified; popularity is not quality |
| Context priors | Recency, revision, field/age-normalized citation context, venue/retraction metadata | Weak capped prior; raw citation/venue/author prestige cannot dominate |
| Diversity/exploration | Source quota, author/topic similarity, controlled novelty | Selection constraint, not an unbounded score bonus |

An initial production weight set is not frozen in this plan. Milestone V020-M7 must choose weights
from the evaluation data under these constraints: interest relevance remains the largest group;
scientific-quality and reproducibility signals cannot jointly override a clearly irrelevant paper;
feedback and identity groups are capped; and inapplicable evidence is excluded rather than scored as
zero. Weight changes produce a new immutable weight-set version and evaluation report.

### LLM fine-ranking contract

The preferred design to benchmark is a two-stage contract:

1. `judge-v1` evaluates only the coarse top K and returns compact typed dimensions, uncertainty, and
   bounded evidence references. It returns no marketing prose and cannot introduce candidate IDs.
2. Local policy combines normalized local features and validated judge dimensions, then applies
   thresholds, diversity, exploration, and target count.
3. `explain-v1` generates summary, recommendation reason, and a concise limitation/uncertainty note
   only for final candidates. Its statements must be attributable to supplied candidate/evidence
   fields and pass length, completeness, and generic-language checks.

The implementation must benchmark this against a single-pass alternative because two passes repeat
some input. The chosen design must satisfy the measured token/cost target, cache judge and explanation
outputs separately, and invalidate only the affected layer when a profile, candidate revision,
evidence snapshot, rubric, model, or output language changes.

### Public evidence enrichment

Public enrichment is optional and failure-tolerant. A provider spike will compare coverage, terms,
latency, rate limits, cost, provenance, and stable identifiers before one adapter is selected. Current
candidates include:

- [OpenAlex Works API](https://developers.openalex.org/api-reference/works/list-works) for topics,
  open-access status, citation context, and retraction/publication metadata.
- [Semantic Scholar Academic Graph API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/api-docs/graph) and
  [Recommendations API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/api-docs/recommendations) for paper identity,
  graph metadata, embeddings/recommendations, and positive/negative seed experiments.
- [GitHub repository metadata](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/repos/repos) for verified public
  repository facts, subject to documented [REST rate limits](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api).

Provider output is untrusted. URLs are allowlisted, responses are size-bounded and validated at the
adapter boundary, public facts have observation timestamps and TTLs, and unavailable enrichment
degrades to `unknown` without failing the batch. Repository README or source text is not executed and
is not sent directly to the model; v0.2.0 starts with structured metadata and bounded artifact checks.

## Human-Curated Quality Corpus

### Recommendation

A small curated corpus has high expected value; a large manually maintained positive-only library
does not. The corpus should primarily calibrate and evaluate the system, with secondary use as strong
local preference exemplars. It should not be treated as enough data to train a general quality model.

The lowest-effort path is to reuse Zotero:

- A dedicated positive collection for papers the user considers genuinely valuable.
- A hard-negative collection for papers that look relevant but should not be recommended.
- Optional structured tags for reason codes such as `novel-insight`, `strong-evidence`,
  `useful-method`, `reproducible`, `clear-writing`, `foundational`, or `not-solid`.
- Optional private free-text rationale in Zotero notes or an ignored local sidecar. Free text remains
  local and is never copied into browser feedback Issues or remote profiles.

### Pilot and expansion rule

Start only after the evaluation schema exists, with approximately 20–30 positives, 10–15 hard
negatives, and 10 pairwise choices near a realistic decision boundary. Include both classics and
recent papers; otherwise the corpus will over-reward age, citation count, or mature topics. Include
at least a few theoretical, empirical, survey, and systems papers so open-source applicability can be
tested rather than assumed.

Expand toward 50–100 labeled papers only when one of these is true:

- Metric confidence is too low to distinguish two ranking versions.
- Error analysis identifies an underrepresented topic or paper type.
- Production false positives/negatives reveal a new boundary case.
- A proposed new weight cannot be evaluated with existing labels.

Adding five well-chosen boundary examples after error analysis is expected to be more valuable than
bulk-labeling easy positives. The evaluator must report corpus composition, label source, time split,
and conflicts so that manual curation does not silently become test leakage.

### Local schema

The local corpus schema v1 will support canonical paper identity, label source, positive/negative or
pairwise judgment, reason codes, applicability flags, optional private rationale, event time, and
schema version. An example fixture is synthetic and tracked; the real corpus lives under ignored
`runtime/` storage with owner-only permissions. Only bounded derived profile facets may cross the
local boundary after explicit profile publication.

## Feedback Learning Design

Feedback schema v2 separates immutable events from derived aggregates:

- `impression`: paper, batch, displayed rank, and exposure time bucket; it has no preference weight.
- `interested` and `save_for_later`: weak positive intent.
- `read`: completion evidence but not necessarily endorsement.
- `worthwhile` or `not_worthwhile`: strong delayed outcome.
- `not_interested`: explicit negative with optional categorical reason such as topic mismatch,
  incremental contribution, weak evidence, poor clarity, or no practical relevance.

Only categorical codes and public paper/run identifiers may travel through GitHub Issues. Free text
is excluded because Issues may be public. Repeated submissions are idempotent; corrections produce a
new event rather than mutating history. Aggregation uses bounded decay and minimum samples, and the
active aggregate snapshot advances only after validation. A single negative does not create a broad
topic ban, and feedback about scientific quality is not conflated with personal relevance.

Position bias is addressed by storing displayed rank and comparing outcomes conditional on exposure.
The evaluator must not treat unclicked or unreviewed impressions as negatives. Online metrics are
reported by weight-set and prompt version so model or ranking changes do not mix incompatible data.

## Efficiency Strategy and Budgets

- Incremental Zotero features are keyed by normalized content hashes and extractor versions; unchanged
  items do no repeated parsing, embedding, or model work.
- The 1,000-paper public pool is reduced by deterministic exclusion, deduplication, and coarse ranking
  before any optional enrichment or LLM call.
- Public enrichment runs only for the bounded top K, uses batched endpoints where available, stores
  revision-aware TTL caches, and respects serialized provider rate limits and retry headers.
- The judge input limit, explanation input limit, candidate count, request count, retry count, output
  tokens, wall time, and estimated cost are explicit configuration with safe ranges.
- Strong cache keys include candidate content/revision, evidence snapshot, protected profile digest,
  feedback aggregate version, rubric/prompt version, model, provider settings, and output language.
- Cache misses and near-cutoff uncertainty drive work. The pipeline must not regenerate explanations
  for unchanged final papers or rejudge candidates whose relevant inputs are unchanged.
- SQLite and bounded JSON snapshots remain sufficient. No vector database is introduced without a
  measured SQLite/local-array bottleneck and a separate approved plan.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Close v0.1.2 formally and capture its immutable benchmark artifacts.
2. Build evaluation and corpus tooling before changing profile, feedback, prompts, or weights.
3. Add characterization tests, then introduce normalized feature and state contracts incrementally.
4. Implement the richer local profile and feedback aggregates without changing production ranking.
5. Replace coarse ranking behind an explicit weight-set version and compare offline.
6. Evaluate external evidence adapters; add only signals with adequate coverage, provenance, and cost.
7. Introduce compact judging and final-only explanation generation; run cache and cost benchmarks.
8. Calibrate with held-out labels, run feature ablations, then execute shadow and canary deployment.
9. Remove temporary production compatibility paths after rollback confidence and complete the release.

## Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sparse or biased feedback | Apparent improvement may reflect rank exposure or a few papers | Record impressions/rank, use explicit outcomes, minimum samples, temporal holdout, and provisional metrics |
| Overfitting a curated corpus | Production novelty and topic coverage decline | Include hard negatives and recent papers, hold out labels, use ablations, cap exemplar influence, add boundary cases only after error analysis |
| Popularity/prestige bias | Older, famous, or well-resourced work dominates | Use field/age normalization, weak caps, applicability, and audits by paper age/type; do not use stars or venue directly as quality proof |
| Open-source signal unfairness | Theory or closed-data domains are penalized | Explicit applicability, `unknown` state, no missing-evidence penalty, separate reproducibility group, paper-type evaluation |
| LLM self-confidence and hallucination | Unsupported novelty/quality claims affect rank or prose | Typed rubric, evidence references, uncertainty caps, local combination, strict validation, no direct model authority over state or URLs |
| Prompt or model drift | Scores become incomparable across batches | Version prompts/rubrics/models, calibrate per version, separate metrics, invalidate caches, require evaluation before activation |
| External metadata mismatch | Wrong repository/citation graph corrupts signals | Canonical ID/DOI matching, verified association, provenance, confidence, TTL, and safe `unknown` fallback |
| API cost/rate limits | Batch latency or failure grows | Top-K enrichment, batching, caching, explicit budgets, retry headers, optional providers, stale-but-valid bounded facts |
| Feedback privacy | Public Issues reveal reading rationale | Categorical codes only, no free text, allowlisted fields, encrypted site, artifact scans |
| Schema complexity | Patch-on-patch compatibility becomes permanent | Exact migrations, one authoritative current model, temporary adapter owners/removal milestone, rollback fixtures |
| Metric gaming | One aggregate improves while useful recommendations worsen | Multi-metric gate, qualitative error sample, feature ablation, negative-rate and diversity guardrails |

## Privacy and Security

- Raw Zotero metadata, notes, annotations, PDF text, curated rationales, and local label history remain
  local. Local storage uses ignored paths and owner-only permissions.
- The protected profile exports only allowlisted, bounded, derived preference facets and versions.
  Any expansion of the trust boundary requires a documented field audit and an ADR.
- Candidate metadata and external evidence are public but still untrusted. HTML, README text, URLs,
  model content, and provider fields are normalized, escaped, allowlisted, and size-bounded.
- Model prompts separate system instructions, preference context, rubric, and quoted candidate data.
  Model responses cannot create identifiers, URLs, weights, checkpoints, or executable actions.
- Logs and manifests contain versions, counts, timings, cache metrics, token/cost summaries, and metric
  aggregates only. They never contain raw prompts, complete profiles, private labels, or paper prose.
- GitHub tokens remain server-side with least privilege. No new secret is embedded in Pages output.

## Persistence and Migration Impact

Expected versioned changes include:

- Local Zotero SQLite migrations for extractor versions, structured item evidence, and optional
  corpus mappings. Existing normalized items remain usable; enrichment is incremental and rebuildable.
- Protected remote-profile v4 with weighted/faceted derived preferences and confidence. Readers for
  v1–v3 remain exact; legacy profiles receive explicit defaults and no invented evidence.
- Feedback event/aggregate schema v2 with a one-way v1 import that preserves old per-paper adjustments
  as legacy evidence rather than pretending they were impression-aware events.
- Ranking snapshot and weight-set schema v1, LLM judge/explanation cache namespaces, recommendation
  schema v3, and publishable-site schema v4 if quality dimensions or evidence badges are exposed.
- Optional public-evidence cache schema v1 keyed by provider, canonical paper/repository identity,
  adapter version, observation time, and TTL.

Every migration is transactional or write-new-and-promote. Old state is retained until the new
snapshot validates and a successful deployment checkpoint is reached. Rebuildable caches are never
the sole source of feedback or label truth.

## Test and Verification Strategy

### Characterization and unit tests

- Freeze v0.1.2 profile, feedback, scoring, quotas, diversity, ordering, cache keys, and empty/failure
  behavior before refactoring.
- Test normalization, applicability, confidence, group caps, decay, minimum samples, negative signals,
  deterministic ties, exploration bounds, and unknown evidence.
- Test corpus conflicts, pairwise labels, temporal splits, metric calculations, insufficient samples,
  calibration, and feature ablations with synthetic fixtures.
- Test prompt contracts, evidence references, personalized-reason specificity, summary completeness,
  token budgets, cache invalidation, retries, and malicious candidate/repository content.

### Contract, integration, and migration tests

- Exact readers and migrations for remote-profile v1–v4, feedback v1–v2, recommendation/publishable
  schemas, local SQLite, evidence cache, and weight sets.
- Mock-server tests for selected evidence providers: timeout, 404, malformed data, wrong identity,
  pagination, rate limit, retry-after, partial data, stale cache, and provider outage.
- End-to-end offline runs from synthetic Zotero/evaluation input through coarse ranking, judging,
  local selection, explanation, encryption, feedback import, and next-snapshot aggregation.
- Repeated-run, partial failure, failed deployment, state promotion, downgrade/rollback, and cache
  rebuild tests.

### Evaluation and production verification

- Record the v0.1.2 baseline distribution for candidates, scores, selected sources, model tokens,
  requests, retries, latency, cache hits, and cost before changing behavior.
- Use temporal holdout where timestamps exist; otherwise isolate curated and production-derived labels
  by paper identity and label time. Never evaluate on labels used to tune the active weight set.
- Compare feature groups through ablation and inspect representative false positives, false negatives,
  high-uncertainty papers, theoretical papers, and repository-missing papers.
- Shadow mode writes a private evaluation artifact but cannot deploy recommendations or advance state.
  Canary activation uses a new immutable weight/prompt version and preserves the v0.1.2 rollback path.
- Run formatter, lint, strict types, all tests, migrations, package build, encrypted/public static
  builds, asset budgets, secret/private-data scans, and an approved cost-bounded production canary.

## Rollback and Recovery

- Keep the immutable v0.1.2 tag, protected profile, successful recommendation history, and last usable
  Pages artifact available throughout the canary.
- Activate profile, feedback aggregate, weight set, prompt set, and evidence snapshot by explicit
  versioned pointers. Rollback changes pointers; it does not rewrite event history or migrate backward.
- If optional enrichment fails, continue with cached valid facts or `unknown` values under the same
  deterministic policy. If required judgment/explanation fails, preserve the previous site and do not
  promote learning or history state.
- A failed migration leaves old files/tables untouched. New caches and derived snapshots can be
  discarded and rebuilt from the local Zotero store, feedback events, and curated labels.
- Remove the v0.1.2 production adapter only after the v0.2.0 canary, rollback rehearsal, and release
  criteria pass; the immutable release tag remains the long-term recovery point.

## Decision Notes

### 2026-08-02 — Evaluation before new weights

- Selected an evaluation-led refactor instead of adding more constants to the current unnormalized
  sum. New signals must expose applicability, confidence, provenance, and a versioned weight set.

### 2026-08-02 — Curated corpus is small and boundary-focused

- A small mixture of positives, hard negatives, and pairwise choices is recommended as an evaluation
  and calibration asset. A large positive-only manual library is deferred because it has lower
  marginal value, encourages overfitting, and creates ongoing maintenance work.

### 2026-08-02 — Open source is evidence, not a universal requirement

- Repository availability is treated as one applicability-aware reproducibility signal. Missing code
  is `unknown` for inapplicable paper types, and popularity metrics cannot dominate relevance or
  scientific-quality assessment.
